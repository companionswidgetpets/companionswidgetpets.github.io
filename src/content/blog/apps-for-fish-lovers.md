If you stop at every aquarium you pass, know which of your friends' fish is which, and once spent twenty minutes watching a jellyfish, this list is for you. These are the iPhone apps that make a fish lover's phone a little more underwater: something to look at, something to learn from, and something to play.

We make the first app on the list, Companions. The rest we picked because they're good at what they do; facts about them come from their App Store listings as of November 2026.

## A tank you can always see

### Companions

Companions keeps a small aquarium in a widget on your Home Screen, and the fish swim all day with the app closed. There are 58 hand-drawn species to choose from, true to their real selves: the clownfish, the regal tang and the Moorish idol of the reef, koi and catfish from the pond, axolotls and frogs, four sharks and a manta ray, whales, an octopus and a seahorse. The starfish creeps along the bed, the shrimp walks it at a scuttle, and the great whales are drawn half again as big as everyone else.

You name each fish, choose its water from eight styles and decorate the bed with corals, ferns and shells. Nothing needs feeding or cleaning, and nothing dies, which makes it the one aquarium that is never a worry. The widget is free in every size with eight fish, and it follows you to StandBy, Apple Watch and your Mac, with an iPad app too.

## Name that fish

### Seek by iNaturalist

Point your camera at a fish, a plant or a mushroom and Seek tells you what it is, then gives you badges for the species you find. Free, made by the team behind iNaturalist, and a joy at an aquarium or a rock pool.

### iNaturalist

For the more serious: share a photo of what you saw, get identification suggestions, and have them confirmed by a worldwide community of naturalists. Your sightings become data that scientists use. Free.

### Picture Fish

Take or upload a photo and Picture Fish suggests which of more than 2,500 species it is, and there is a community of fish keepers to ask. Free to try, with a premium plan.

### Fishbrain

Made for anglers, with fishing maps, catches logged by its community and a fish identifier for hundreds of species. Free, with subscriptions.

## Look after a real aquarium

If you keep real fish, a tracker helps with the part nobody loves: remembering when you last tested the water. These are newer, smaller apps, so read the recent reviews first.

- **AquaLog (Aquarium Tank Tracker)** logs water tests and changes, sends reminders, and keeps a photo timeline so you can compare your tank on day one with today.
- **Fishkeeper**, from the UK aquatics retailer Maidenhead Aquatics, keeps records for any number of aquariums and ponds.
- **App-aquatic** combines a tank log with help working out what is wrong with a sick fish.

## Watch real fish, live

- **Monterey Bay Aquarium live cams.** The California aquarium streams its exhibits on [its website](https://www.montereybayaquarium.org/animals/live-cams): the kelp forest, the open sea, jellies, sharks, a coral reef, sea otters and more. Most cams run from 7 a.m. to 7 p.m. Pacific time, so they are live in Europe's evening and Asia's morning.
- **Georgia Aquarium webcams.** [Nine cams](https://www.georgiaaquarium.org/webcam/) in Atlanta, including Ocean Voyager, its enormous ocean tank, and the beluga whales.
- **Aquarium Live – Real Fish Tank** plays looping videos of real reef tanks with ocean sounds and a sleep timer: a fish-tank screensaver for your phone.

## Fishy games

- **Tap Tap Fish – AbyssRium** is an idle game: tap to bring a deep-sea tank to life and fill it with fish, corals and the odd whale. Free, with ads and in-app purchases.
- **Fishdom** is a match-three puzzle game where every level you win pays for decorations for your fish's tanks. Free, with in-app purchases.

## Eating fish, kindly

Monterey Bay Aquarium's **Seafood Watch**, the guide to which fish are fished or farmed sustainably, has moved from an app to [its website](https://www.seafoodwatch.org/). Save it to your Home Screen from Safari's share menu and it opens like an app.

## Questions people ask

### Is there an app that identifies fish from a photo?

Yes. Seek by iNaturalist and iNaturalist identify fish (and most other living things) from a photo for free. Picture Fish specialises in fish, with more than 2,500 species, and Fishbrain covers the fish anglers catch.

### Can I watch an aquarium live online?

Yes. Monterey Bay Aquarium and Georgia Aquarium both stream their exhibits on their websites, from kelp forests to beluga whales.

### Is there a fish tank widget for iPhone?

Yes: Companions puts a small aquarium on your Home Screen where the fish swim all day, free in small, medium and large. We compared it with the others in [the best aquarium widget apps](/blog/best-aquarium-widget-apps-iphone/).

### Why do fish lovers find aquariums so calming?

There is research behind it: people watching an aquarium show lower heart rates and better moods, and more fish seem to help. We wrote about it in [why watching fish is so relaxing](/blog/why-watching-fish-is-relaxing/).
