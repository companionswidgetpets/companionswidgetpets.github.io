There's a reason doctors' waiting rooms and restaurants have fish tanks, and a reason you can lose ten minutes watching one. Watching fish really does calm people down, and researchers have measured it. Here is what they found, why it might work, and how to keep a little of that calm within reach.

## What the research found

### Heart rate falls, mood rises

The best-known study was done at the National Marine Aquarium in Plymouth, England, and [published in 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5081108/). Researchers measured people's heart rate and blood pressure, and asked about their mood, while they sat in front of a 550,000-litre exhibit tank. Watching it lowered blood pressure and heart rate and lifted people's mood.

The clever part: the tank was being restocked at the time, so the team could compare it empty, partly stocked and full, with 138 fish of 19 species. More fish held people's attention for longer, made them more interested and lifted their mood further, and heart rate fell more once there were fish to watch. Even the empty tank of water and rocks lowered blood pressure a little.

### Calmer at the dentist

Back in 1984, a study of patients about to have oral surgery found that those who spent a while watching an aquarium beforehand were more relaxed during the operation than those who looked at a poster, and as relaxed as those who had been hypnotised.

### Calmer at mealtimes

Aquariums have been tried in care homes too. In a study of people living with Alzheimer's disease, residents ate more after an aquarium was placed in their dining room.

## Why it might work

Nobody knows for certain, but a few ideas fit:

- **Soft fascination.** Psychologists use the term for things that hold your attention gently, like clouds, flames or moving water, letting the busy, focused part of your mind rest.
- **Movement without urgency.** Fish move constantly but never demand anything. There is always something to watch and nothing to do about it.
- **Water, and blue and green.** A large body of research links time near water, sometimes called "blue space", with lower stress. An aquarium brings a small piece of it indoors.

## Questions people ask

### Does watching fish reduce stress?

Studies suggest it does: watching an aquarium has been found to lower heart rate and blood pressure, lift mood and help people stay relaxed during dental surgery.

### Why do doctors and dentists have fish tanks?

Because they help nervous patients relax while they wait, an effect researchers measured as far back as the 1980s.

### Are more fish more relaxing?

In the Plymouth study, yes: a better-stocked tank held attention longer and lifted mood more than a sparse one.

### Is a virtual aquarium relaxing?

It hasn't been studied, so no one can say for sure. But many people find a slow, gentle aquarium on their phone a pleasant thing to glance at, and unlike a real one, it never needs cleaning.
