In 1996 a little egg-shaped keyring from Japan made a whole generation responsible for a pixel creature that beeped when it was hungry. Thirty years on, virtual pets have moved to the iPhone Home Screen, the Lock Screen and even the Dynamic Island, where you see them every time you pick up your phone.

They differ most in one thing: what they ask of you. Some need feeding, washing and walking, and get sad if you forget. Others are simply there, keeping you company. Here are the best of both, so you can choose the kind of pet you actually want.

Full disclosure: we make Companions, the first one on the list. Everything about the others comes from their App Store listings as of November 2026.

## Two kinds of widget pet

- **Pets that need you.** Feed them, play with them, keep their mood up. Satisfying if you like a daily ritual; a small weight on your conscience on a busy week.
- **Pets that keep you company.** Nothing to keep up, nothing to lose. You look, you smile, you carry on with your day.

Both are lovely. It helps to know which you're signing up for.

## 1. Companions: fish that can never die

Companions puts a small aquarium in a Home Screen widget, and the fish in it really swim, all day, with the app closed. You choose them from 58 hand-drawn species, from neon tetras to an axolotl, a frog and a blue whale, give each one a name, and arrange their tank with water styles and props.

It is firmly a company pet. The fish don't get hungry, don't get sick and never die; leave for a month and they are exactly where you left them. Feeding is there as a little game for when you feel like it: pinch some flakes into the water and they chase them. Some mornings a fish says hello as the app opens.

The widget is free in small, medium and large, with eight fish, every water style and every prop. The same tank goes to StandBy, Apple Watch and your Mac, and there is an iPad app too. No account, no ads, no tracking, and no weekly fee to keep a pet alive. That last one was the reason we made it.

## 2. Pixel Pals: pets in the Dynamic Island

Pixel Pals puts pixel-art pets in the Dynamic Island and Live Activities, as well as on the Home Screen, the Lock Screen and in StandBy ("so you can count sheep as you sleep", says its listing). The dog and the cat are free; Premium adds more animals. They eat farm food, and affection unlocks new forms. Its App Store privacy label says it collects no data.

**Best for:** a pet that walks along the top of your screen.

## 3. Widgetable: a pet you raise with friends

Widgetable is a social widget app: you and a friend or partner share widgets on each other's Home Screens, including a pet you raise together. Feed it, play with it and watch it grow. It is free with a premium subscription, and its listing says it contains ads.

**Best for:** couples and best friends who like sharing small things.

## 4. Mochi Pets: a classic pocket pet

Mochi Pets is the closest to the original keyring pets: a cute pixel animal in an interactive widget that you feed, pet, bathe and put to bed. Look after it and it's happy; neglect it and its listing warns it might get grumpy or sad. It shows in StandBy too. Free, with an optional subscription for more pets and themes.

**Best for:** people who miss the 1990s.

## 5. Pixel Pets: a pet that wants walks

Pixel Pets has animated pixel pets on the Home Screen, the Lock Screen, the Dynamic Island and in StandBy. They need feeding every day and taking out for exercise, and their mood shows how you're doing.

**Best for:** pairing a pet with your own daily walk.

## 6. Finch: a pet that grows when you look after yourself

In Finch, you have a little bird who grows as you take care of yourself, one small self-care goal at a time. It is less a game than a gentle habit tracker with a friend in it, and it has a widget.

**Best for:** building small habits without a to-do list.

## 7. Plant Nanny: a pet that is a plant

Plant Nanny is a water tracker: every glass you drink waters a little plant, which grows as you stay hydrated. Free with ads, or a subscription without.

**Best for:** people who forget to drink water.

## What about Tamagotchi itself?

Bandai has two Tamagotchi games on the App Store, but neither is a widget. **My Tamagotchi Forever** is a classic care game you open to feed, wash and clean up after your pet, and **Tamagotchi Adventure Kingdom** is an adventure game on Apple Arcade. For a pet that lives on your Home Screen, the apps above are the modern take.

## Home Screen, Lock Screen or Dynamic Island?

- **Home Screen** widgets are the biggest and the most colourful: room for a whole tank or a pet with its house.
- **Lock Screen** widgets are small and drawn in a single tint, good for a tiny pixel pet but not for a full-colour aquarium.
- **The Dynamic Island** and Live Activities let a pet wander along the top of the screen, even inside other apps.
- **StandBy**, when your iPhone charges on its side, shows widgets large, beside the clock. A swimming tank makes a lovely bedside companion; see [our StandBy guide](/blog/standby/).

A pet on your phone should make you feel good when you look at it, not guilty when you didn't. So in Companions, nothing ever depends on you. The fish simply swim.

## Questions people ask

### How do I get a pet on my iPhone Home Screen?

Install a pet widget app and open it once, then touch and hold an empty part of the Home Screen, tap **Edit**, then **Add Widget**, find the app and choose a size. Our [widget guide](/blog/add-the-widget/) walks through it.

### Is there a Tamagotchi widget for iPhone?

Bandai's own Tamagotchi apps aren't widgets, but Mochi Pets and Pixel Pets work in the same spirit, with pets you feed and look after. If you would rather have the company without the care, Companions' fish never need feeding.

### Which pet widget never dies?

In Companions the fish never die, whatever you do or don't do. Feeding is optional and only for fun.

### Can I have a pet on my Lock Screen?

Yes, with Pixel Pals, Pixel Pets or Widgetable, drawn in the Lock Screen's single tint. Companions keeps its fish in full colour on the Home Screen and in StandBy.
