The short answer: not quite. An iPhone wallpaper can't play an animation all day, and no app is allowed to set one. But there is a way to have fish swimming on your Home Screen all day long, and it looks even better than a wallpaper would.

## What iPhone wallpapers can and can't do

- **Live Photo wallpapers** move, but only on the Lock Screen and only for a moment: the photo plays in slow motion when you wake your iPhone, then stops. Not every Live Photo qualifies, only ones iOS can turn into slow motion.
- **Spatial scenes** (iOS 26, on iPhone 12 and later) give a photo a 3D look that shifts as you tilt your phone. Beautiful, but nothing swims.
- **Animated wallpapers that play all day don't exist on iPhone.** Apple doesn't let apps set or animate your wallpaper. So-called live wallpaper apps can only save a Live Photo for you to set yourself, and it plays for a second on the Lock Screen, if at all.

## The way that works: an aquarium widget

A widget sits on top of your wallpaper, on the Home Screen, and a few widgets can animate. In Companions, the fish swim continuously, turning, gliding and letting out the odd bubble, all day, with the app closed. Put a big tank over a matching wallpaper and you have, for all practical purposes, a living aquarium on your Home Screen.

And it doesn't cost battery the way a video would. The fish move the same way iOS sweeps a clock widget's hands, with nothing running in the background; [how the fish really swim](/blog/how-the-widget-animates/) explains it.

## How to make it look like a living wallpaper

<ol class="steps">
<li>Install Companions and add a few fish. Big, graceful ones look best: a Sea turtle, a Manta, Jellyfish.</li>
<li>Choose an underwater wallpaper: a photo of the sea, a deep-blue gradient, or a picture you took at an aquarium.</li>
<li>Touch and hold the Home Screen, tap <strong>Edit</strong>, then <strong>Customise</strong>, and choose <strong>Clear</strong>.</li>
<li>Tap <strong>Edit</strong> again, then <strong>Add Widget</strong>, find <strong>Companions</strong> and add the <strong>large</strong> size at the top of the screen.</li>
</ol>

Here is the trick in step 3: with the Home Screen set to Clear, iOS swaps the tank's water for its own clear glass, and the fish and props swim over it in their own colours. So your wallpaper shows through the tank, and the fish appear to swim right across it.

Prefer colour to glass? Leave the icons on **Default** and choose a water style to match your wallpaper instead: Twilight for a night-sea wallpaper, Minimal for a bright blue one, Pond for green.

## Make it fill the screen

- **Use two tanks.** Each widget size is its own tank with its own fish, so a large tank at the top and a medium one below it make a two-level reef.
- **Keep the icons out of the way.** Put your apps on the second page, or in the Dock, and give the first page to the fish.
- **Bring it to bed.** In StandBy, the small tank sits beside the clock all night while your iPhone charges. Here's [how to set it up](/blog/standby/).

## Questions people ask

### Can I set a moving wallpaper on iPhone?

Only a Live Photo, which plays briefly in slow motion on the Lock Screen when you wake the iPhone. For movement that lasts, use an animated widget like Companions over your wallpaper.

### Why doesn't my live wallpaper move?

Live Photo wallpapers only play on the Lock Screen, when you wake the phone, and only if iOS can turn the photo into slow motion.

### Is there a fish wallpaper that moves on iPhone?

Not as a wallpaper, but the Companions widget puts real swimming fish on your Home Screen. Set the icons to Clear and the fish swim over your wallpaper itself.

### Does an aquarium widget drain the battery?

Not Companions: its fish are animated by iOS itself, the way a clock widget's hands move, so nothing runs in the background. See [does Companions drain my battery?](/help/battery/)
