An aquarium widget puts a small fish tank on your iPhone Home Screen, so there is something alive to look at every time you unlock your phone. There are more of them than ever, and they are surprisingly different: some fish really swim, some are a picture that changes now and then, some are free and some want a weekly subscription before the tank appears at all.

We make one of these apps, Companions, so read this knowing that. We have tried to be fair to the others, and everything we say about them comes from their own App Store listings and websites as they stood in November 2026.

## What to look for in an aquarium widget

Four questions sort the good ones from the rest.

- **Do the fish actually move on the Home Screen?** A widget isn't an app: iOS draws it from pictures the app prepares in advance, and most widgets stand still between updates. An App Store preview video often shows the app itself rather than the widget, so look for the words "swim on your Home Screen", and read a few reviews.
- **Is the widget itself free?** Several apps are free to download but keep the Home Screen tank behind a subscription. Check what the free version actually puts on your Home Screen.
- **Does it want looking after?** Some tanks need feeding, cleaning and a happiness meter kept up. That is a game some people love and others find one more thing to feel guilty about.
- **Where else does it go?** StandBy on your bedside table, an Apple Watch, an iPad, a Mac desktop.

## 1. Companions: a tank that swims all day, for free

Companions was built around a single idea: the fish should swim on your Home Screen, continuously, with the app closed. Most widgets can't do that, because iOS doesn't let an app run while its widget is on screen. Companions gets round it with the one kind of motion iOS performs on its own, the steady turn of a clock widget's hands: each fish's path is built from a few such turning hands, so iOS animates it without the app and without draining your battery. [How the fish really swim](/blog/how-the-widget-animates/) explains the trick.

What you get for free:

- the widget in all three sizes, and in StandBy
- eight fish: the Tang, Sardine and Puffer every tank starts with, and the Freshwater Pack
- all eight water styles, all nineteen props, My Tanks for keeping a shelf of designs, and eighteen ready-made tanks on the Explore page

There are 58 hand-drawn species in all, in twelve packs. The others are one-time purchases, or all together with All Fish, which also adds a clock, the date, the weather, your step goal or the Moon phase in a corner of the tank. Each widget size is its own tank with its own five fish, so a small reef and a large whale tank can share a Home Screen.

There is nothing to look after. Feeding exists because it's fun (pinch some flakes into the water and watch them chase it), but nobody goes hungry if you never do. There is no account, no advertising and no tracking, and the app speaks twelve languages. The same tank shows on Apple Watch and, through iPhone widgets, on a Mac desktop, and there is a full Companions app for iPad too.

What it doesn't do: Companions lives on the Home Screen, not the Lock Screen, whose widgets are drawn in a single tint that would turn the fish into grey outlines. StandBy shows the tank in full colour instead. It needs iOS 26 or later.

## 2. Aquarium Widgets: for people who like a care game

Aquarium Widgets turns the tank into a game. You build tanks from themed fish packs, feed the fish, clean the glass and keep their happiness up, and objectives and levels unlock more. It can show a clock or a month calendar behind the fish, and on iOS 27 it offers an extra-large tank that holds up to twelve fish.

The app is free to download, but its own site says that showing an aquarium as a Home Screen widget requires the premium upgrade, sold weekly, yearly or for life. If looking after a tank is the point for you, it is well made. If the idea of fish waiting to be fed sounds like one more notification, it's the opposite of what we wanted from a Home Screen pet.

## 3. ReefPocket: a free reef with a Lock Screen widget

ReefPocket is a calm offline reef. Its widgets, in small, medium and large and on the Lock Screen, are free, and extra fish come in one-time packs with no subscription. You can tap to drop food, and the developer promises that your fish never die. A good pick if you want a reef on your Lock Screen as well as your Home Screen.

## 4. Glass Aquarium: glassy bowls to decorate

Glass Aquarium, new this year for iOS 26, lets you decorate liquid-glass bowls and tanks, with an optional pet mode for feeding and keeping fish happy. Its developer has explained that its widgets show the bowl as a still picture, so the movement happens inside the app. Lovely to look at; just don't expect the fish to swim on your Home Screen.

## 5. bigsmall: a pixel-art tank in a widget kit

bigsmall is a set of widgets (countdowns, calendars, clock faces) that includes a transparent pixel-art tank with swimming cats, fish and ducks. Worth a look if you want one app for a whole retro-styled Home Screen rather than an aquarium first.

## Fishy apps that aren't widgets

- **Aquarium Live** plays looping videos of real reef tanks with ocean sounds and a sleep timer. It is an app you open, a little like a screensaver for your phone.
- **Tap Tap Fish – AbyssRium** is an idle game: tap to grow a deep-sea tank full of fish and the odd whale.

## How to add an aquarium widget

Whichever app you choose, the steps are the same: open the app once, touch and hold an empty part of your Home Screen, tap **Edit**, then **Add Widget**, find the app, choose a size and tap **Add Widget**. Our [step-by-step guide](/blog/add-the-widget/) has the details, and what to do if the widget doesn't show up.

## So which one?

If you want fish that really swim, all day, free in every size, that never need you and never die, that is exactly what Companions is for. If you want a tank that keeps you busy, Aquarium Widgets is built around that. And whichever you choose, give it a corner of the Home Screen you look at often: an aquarium is better seen than kept.

## Questions people ask

### Is there a free aquarium widget for iPhone?

Yes. Companions puts its tank on your Home Screen for free in small, medium and large, with eight fish, every water style and every prop. ReefPocket's widgets are free too. Some other aquarium apps are free to download but charge for the widget itself, so check before you subscribe.

### Do aquarium widgets drain the battery?

They shouldn't. A widget is drawn by iOS, not run by the app. Companions animates its fish the way iOS sweeps a clock widget's hands, so nothing runs in the background. More in [does Companions drain my battery?](/help/battery/)

### Can the fish die?

In Companions, never. Leave it for a month and the fish are exactly where you left them, swimming. In care-based apps, fish may need regular feeding and cleaning to stay happy.

### Can I put an aquarium on my Lock Screen?

Lock Screen widgets are small and drawn in a single tint, so a few apps offer a simple tank there. Companions keeps its fish in full colour on the Home Screen and in StandBy, which shows the tank while your iPhone charges on its side.
