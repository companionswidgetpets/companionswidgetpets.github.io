StandBy turns your iPhone into a little bedside display. Lay it on its side on a charger and the Lock Screen gives way to a big clock, your photos, or two widgets side by side. Choose those two well and your phone becomes the nicest thing on the bedside table.

## How StandBy works

StandBy appears when your iPhone is **charging, on its side, still and locked**. Turn it on in **Settings › StandBy**. Swipe sideways to move between its three views: a pair of widgets, your photos, and a full-screen clock.

The widget view holds two stacks, left and right. Each widget is shown large, filling half the screen, so a small widget becomes a big one. Swipe up or down on either side to move through a stack.

On iPhones with an always-on display, StandBy can stay visible all night. On the others it wakes when you tap the screen or nudge the table. In a dark room, **Night Mode** dims the screen and turns it red so it doesn't light up the bedroom.

## The best widgets for StandBy

### 1. Companions: an aquarium for your bedside

A tank of fish swimming slowly next to the clock is the most calming thing StandBy can show, and that's exactly what Companions puts there. The fish swim all the time, with the app closed and without draining the battery, and you can wake up to them.

Choose the **Dark** style for the night: it is as black as the screen allows, so the tank sits quietly in the dark rather than glowing. Bright fish show best in it, like the Neon, the Jellyfish and the Ghost. StandBy shows your small tank, and the widget is free. [Our StandBy guide](/blog/standby/) walks through the setup.

### 2. Clock

The obvious partner. Apple's Clock widget comes in an analogue face, a world clock for family abroad, and a list of your alarms, so you can check that tomorrow's is set before you sleep.

### 3. Weather

Glance over in the morning and know whether you need a coat. A big, clear forecast is one of StandBy's best uses.

### 4. Calendar

Your first meeting of the day, or the date and a count of what's coming, in large type you can read from bed.

### 5. Batteries

Charging your AirPods and Apple Watch overnight too? The Batteries widget shows them all filling up together.

### 6. Photos

A widget of favourite photos beside the clock, or swipe to StandBy's own photo view to fill the whole screen.

### 7. Home

If you have smart lights or plugs, the Home widget keeps them a tap away from bed.

### 8. Pixel Pals

Pixel-art pets that can sit in StandBy, so that, in its own words, you can count sheep as you sleep.

## Live Activities take over

Some things don't need a widget. A running timer, a delivery on its way or a match score shows as a Live Activity, and in StandBy it fills the screen while it's happening.

## Tips for a better StandBy

1. **Pick two that work together.** A clock or the weather on one side, something lovely on the other. A swimming tank beside a big clock is our favourite.
2. **Turn off Smart Rotate** if you want your tank to stay put. Touch and hold the stack, tap **Edit Stack**, and turn off **Smart Rotate**, which otherwise swaps widgets through the day.
3. **Decide about Night Mode.** It keeps the room dark, but tints everything red. If you'd rather see colours, turn it off in **Settings › StandBy** and use dark widgets instead.
4. **Use a stand.** Any charger works, but a stand that holds the iPhone sideways makes StandBy part of the room.

## Questions people ask

### What widgets work in StandBy?

Small widgets from apps that support StandBy, shown large, two at a time. When you add one in StandBy, iOS lists the apps whose widgets can go there.

### Can I put an aquarium in StandBy?

Yes. Touch and hold one of StandBy's widgets, tap **+**, choose **Companions**, then **Add Widget**. Your small tank appears beside the other widget, fish swimming.

### Why is StandBy red?

That's Night Mode, which tints StandBy red in a dark room. Turn it off in **Settings › StandBy**. More fixes in [StandBy not working?](/blog/standby-not-working/)

### Does StandBy drain the battery?

Your iPhone is charging while StandBy is on, so the battery fills regardless. On always-on iPhones, iOS dims the screen when the room is dark and nothing moves.
